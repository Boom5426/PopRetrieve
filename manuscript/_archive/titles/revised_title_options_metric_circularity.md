# Revised Title Options — Metric-Circularity Positioning

Ten options in the repositioned direction: DART as a probe that exposes
evaluation sensitivity / metric-dependent gains in distributional inverse
retrieval. Ordered from most to least conservative on the methodological-audit
framing.

## Preferred (methodological-audit framing)

1. **Objective-aligned metrics can overstate distributional retrieval gains in
   heterogeneous single-cell drug ranking**
2. **Evaluation circularity in distributional inverse retrieval for heterogeneous
   single-cell populations**
3. **Metric-dependent gains in distribution-aware retrieval for heterogeneous
   single-cell drug ranking**
4. **When is distributional information trustworthy? A metric-circularity audit of
   inverse single-cell drug retrieval**

## Probe / instrument framing

5. **DART: a distributional retrieval probe for evaluation sensitivity in
   heterogeneous inverse drug ranking**
6. **DART exposes metric-dependent gains in heterogeneous inverse drug retrieval**
7. **A probe for evaluation circularity in single-cell inverse drug retrieval:
   objective fidelity versus independent utility**

## Unification-forward framing

8. **Mean-signature retrieval is a collapsed special case of distributional
   retrieval — and why that matters for evaluation**
9. **From signatures to distributions: unifying single-cell drug retrieval and
   auditing its evaluation**

## Concise / high-level

10. **Objective fidelity is not independent utility: an evaluation audit of
    distributional single-cell drug retrieval**

---

**Recommended:** Option 1 or 3 for a methods venue (Cell Reports Methods / PLOS
Computational Biology / Bioinformatics) — they lead with the transferable
methodological finding (metric-dependence) rather than the tool name, which suits
the repositioning. Option 5 if the venue prefers a named-method title.

**Avoided throughout** (per spec): "DART improves therapeutic ranking," "precision
medicine," "drug discovery breakthrough," "predicts resistance," "universal
recommender," and any therapeutic-superiority phrasing.
